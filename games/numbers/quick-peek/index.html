Seeing how many without counting is called subitising. Children can usually see up to 3 or 4 at a glance by age 4, and up to 5 by age 5; bigger amounts are seen as parts, like "5 and 2", or as full ten-frames ("ten, twenty, and three more"). The peek is too short to count one by one, which is the point. There are always three numbers to choose from, never next to each other. After an answer the dots come back so children can check. Every 5 right answers earns a star, and each star shortens the peek.
